Power Supply Module

A standalone Regulated DC Power Supply is essential for circuits that require more current or different voltages than the Arduino can provide. In hobby electronics, these often sit directly on a breadboard to provide 3.3V or 5V rails.

How It Works

The power supply converts an unstable input voltage (from a battery or wall adapter) into a constant, stable output voltage:

  1. Voltage Regulation: Uses a linear regulator (like the AMS1117) to drop the input voltage down to a precise 5V or 3.3V.
  2. Filtering: Capacitors on the board smooth out electrical "noise" and ripples, ensuring your sensors and microcontrollers receive clean power.
  3. Protection: Many modules include a fuse or diode to protect your circuit from reverse polarity or short circuits.

Hardware Pinout

Pin / Connector Function
DC Barrel JackMain Input. Typically accepts 7V to 12V DC.
VCC (Output)Positive rail. Configurable to 3.3V or 5V in the properties panel.
GND (Output)Negative rail (Ground).

Using the Emulator

💡 Simulation Testing

In OpenHW Studio, the Power Supply behaves like a real bench power supply. You can change its output voltage by clicking on it and modifying the 'voltage' attribute in the panel. The emulator will automatically simulate the correct power levels for all connected components!

⚠️ Pro-Tip: Common Ground

Always remember to connect the GND of your external Power Supply to the GND of your Arduino. This ensures they share the same reference point, preventing erratic behavior in your sensors and actuators.
