Resistor

A Resistor is a fundamental passive electronic component that implements electrical resistance as a circuit element. In electronic circuits, resistors are used to reduce current flow, adjust signal levels, and divide voltages.

How It Works

Resistors are built using semi-conductive materials (like carbon or metal film) that impede the flow of electrons:

  1. Ohm's Law: The relationship between voltage (V), current (I), and resistance (R) is defined by V = I × R. By increasing the resistance, you decrease the current for a given voltage.
  2. Power Dissipation: As resistance "wastes" electrical energy to slow it down, that energy is converted into heat. Resistors are rated by their wattage (e.g., 1/4W, 1W) to show how much heat they can safely handle.
  3. Color Coding: Most standard resistors use colored bands to indicate their value (in Ohms) and tolerance.
